# CEED BP1 / BP3 on cell-wise storage with flexible CG

The flexible CG on cell-wise vectors of Wichrowski, "Coalesced Matrix-Free Finite Elements
in Cell-Wise Storage" (arXiv 2607.02335, Algorithm 1), with the CEED benchmark operators
generated by MARSIR from four-line specs.

- Operators: `marsir-compiler/specs/bp1.op` (mass, `v = g0*u`) and `bp3.op` (Laplacian,
  `dv = G du`, G the symmetric w det J J^-1 J^-T), `form = galerkin`, q = p + 2 Gauss
  points; PTX from `marsir-mlir/test/bp_gpu_pipeline.py` (cell-wise schedule), CPU gate
  `marsir-mlir/tools/emu_bp.cpp` against direct quadrature sums.
- Solver: `backend/distributed/unstructured/solvers/mars_cellwise_fcg.hpp`. x, p, z are
  continuous, r and q = A p unassembled; every scalar is a plain sum of a dual vector
  times a primal one (the paper's Lemma 1), so the DSS appears only in the
  preconditioner (DSS + Jacobi). It is not free of communication: on several ranks each
  iteration makes two blocking `MPI_Allreduce` calls (one scalar, then two;
  `FcgReduction::finish`).
- Driver: `examples/distributed/unstructured/mars_marsir_bp.cu` (one or several GPUs).
  Reference: `marsir-mlir/test/cellwise_bp_ref.py`.
- PASS: since 2026-10-31 the driver and `mfem_bp3` print PASS only if the solver reached
  `--tol` within `--maxit` and the true assembled residual is finite and at most
  `--true-tol` (default 1e-10), besides the checks they made before (max |x - u| <= 1e-8
  max |u| for b = A u; in MARS also the operator oracles). The PASS marks in the sections below are from before
  this gate; their true residuals are listed with them. Many runs at tol 1e-10 have true
  residuals above 1e-10 (up to 1.9e-9). In the three equal-accuracy batches (tol 3e-13) the
  largest are 1.05e-10, 1.05e-10 and 1.06e-10, just above the default.

## Correctness

numpy: cell-wise FCG and FCG on the assembled system give the same iterates (6e-16 over
25 iterations, BP1 and BP3); BP1 applied to 1 sums to the volume, BP3 applied to 1 is 0,
the BP3 element matrix is symmetric (1e-16), and the solve converges spectrally to the
manufactured solution (BP3 error 1e-4 at p = 3, 9e-6 at p = 5).

GPU (GH200, cstone 3a417b83): the FCG history equals the numpy one to every printed
digit (BP1 p = 3: 17 iterations; BP3 p = 3, 7, 1: 45, 71, 17), and every run passes
its checks (volume, null space, first elements against host quadrature, x = u).

GH200, one GPU, 16.8M element-local DoFs at every degree, deform 0.05, unique DoFs
(ne p + 1)^3. GB/s counts u, y and the stored geometry (1 value per Gauss point for BP1,
6 for BP3).

|---|---|---|---|

| BP3 operator | 1.00 (1384 GB/s) | 6.55 (1678) | 5.37 (667) |
| DSS (unstructured gather) | 1.32 (165 GB/s) | 22.0 (821) | 91.0 (2145) |
| BP1 operator + DSS | 1.02 | 10.3 | 13.2 |

| FCG + DSS-Jacobi iterations, BP1 / BP3 | 18 / 307 | 6 / 770 | 6 / 1225 |
| FCG iteration, BP1 / BP3 | 2.84 / 4.44 ms | 1.48 / 2.07 ms | 1.60 / 2.89 ms |

The paper (A100, 2.0 TB/s) reports for the whole mass operator on a general geometry
with its unstructured DSS path (Table 7): 2.7 / 12.3 GDoF/s at p = 1 / 3, and 16.0 at
p = 6. Our BP1 operator + DSS is below that on a GPU with about twice the bandwidth, so
there is headroom:

- DSS at p = 1: 165 GB/s. The hand unstructured gather runs one warp per element, and a
  p = 1 element has 8 values.
- The p = 7 operators: 627-667 GB/s. The generic cell-wise schedule keeps every sweep
  stage in shared memory (BP3: 42 KB per element), one warp per element.
- FCG: the iteration is 1.7-2x the operator + DSS. The vector updates and pairings are
  separate passes.
- BP3 iterations grow with the mesh and the degree under Jacobi, as expected; a
  multigrid preconditioner is the companion paper's step.

## Geometry: stored per Gauss point against trilinear from the corners

`--spec bp1_tri | bp3_tri` (9bbb398c): the kernel reads the element's 8 corners and
forms w det J (BP1) or w det J J^-1 J^-T (BP3) in registers at each Gauss point. Same
mesh and sizes as above (16.8M cell-wise, GH200). Every run PASS; the host oracle (from
the stored G) agrees to rounding, and iteration counts and histories match the stored
runs (BP3 final residuals differ in the 4th digit).

| operator, ms | p = 1 stored | p = 1 trilinear | p = 3 stored | p = 3 trilinear | p = 7 stored | p = 7 trilinear |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BP1 | 0.466 (1548 GB/s) | 1.005 | 0.383 (1384 GB/s) | 0.605 | 0.730 | 0.712 |
| BP3 | 2.153 (1387 GB/s) | 2.045 | 1.082 (1701 GB/s) | 1.316 | 2.140 | 2.012 |

Trilinear is faster only for BP3 at p = 1 and p = 7 (5-6%) and BP1 at p = 7 (2%), and
slower elsewhere (BP1 p = 1 2.2x). With the geometry gone the trilinear kernels move
137-668 GB/s, so they are no longer limited by bandwidth: the per-point geometry costs
more than reading it. The PTX shows the geometry adds about 170 (BP1) and 230 (BP3)
f64 instructions to the pointwise loop, all recomputed at every point, including the
corner differences that are constant for the element. What it saves is memory: G is
E 6 q^3 doubles for BP3 (2.7 GB at p = 1, 1.6 GB at p = 3) against 24 doubles per
element (0.4 GB, 50 MB).

### 21 per-element coefficients (02fb73e3)

The Jacobian of the trilinear map needs only 7 coefficients per component (the mixed
ones are shared by the three derivatives). A scalar flux computes these 21 from the
corners once per element, in every lane, and each J entry is then 3 FMAs at a point.
Phases, shared memory and warps per block are the same as stored at every p; no spills.
Same runs as above, one session for stored and the 21-coefficient form:

| operator, ms | stored | corners (9bbb398c) | 21 coefficients | registers stored / 21 coef. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BP1 p = 1 | 0.467 | 1.005 | 0.691 | 40 / 80 |
| BP1 p = 3 | 0.382 | 0.605 | 0.473 | 64 / 96 |
| BP1 p = 7 | 0.730 | 0.712 | 0.685 | 212 / 212 |
| BP3 p = 1 | 2.156 | 2.045 | 1.710 | 56 / 114 |
| BP3 p = 3 | 1.109 | 1.316 | 1.138 | 72 / 126 |
| BP3 p = 7 | 2.132 | 2.012 | 1.932 | 206 / 206 |

The coefficient form is faster than the corner form in every case. Against stored geometry,
it is faster for BP3 at p = 1 (21%) and p = 7 (9%) and BP1 at p = 7 (6%), equal within 3%
for BP3 at p = 3, and slower for BP1 at p = 1 (1.48x) and p = 3 (1.24x), where the stored
geometry is one value per point. FCG iteration, BP3: 4.45 -> 3.99 ms (p = 1), 2.07 -> 2.09
(p = 3), 2.89 -> 2.70 (p = 7). At p <= 3 the trilinear kernels still use about twice the
registers; whether that limits them is not measured.

## DSS: block-structured (passes inside blocks + block skeleton)

`--block B --rotate --block-dss` (227bf5a8, `solvers/mars_cellwise_block_dss.hpp`): the
elements stored in blocks of B^3, every block in a pseudo-random one of the 24 proper
rotations; a pass per axis inside the blocks (z out of place, x and y in place), then the
block skeleton from the unstructured tables built over the blocks. Same 16.8M cell-wise
vectors, BP1, GH200. Every run PASS; the block DSS equals the hand gather on a random
vector to 1.8e-15 relative, and iteration counts and histories match.

| DSS, ms | hand gather | block, B = 8 | block, B = 32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = 1 (ne 128) | 1.640 (164 GB/s) | 0.356 | 0.292 (64 blocks) |
| p = 3 (ne 64) | 0.320 | 0.259 | 0.237 (8 blocks) |
| p = 7 (ne 32) | 0.129 (2079 GB/s) | 0.177 | 0.157 (1 block, no skeleton) |

FCG iteration, BP1: p = 1 2.85 -> 1.43 ms (B = 32), p = 3 1.48 -> 1.38, p = 7 1.61 -> 1.63.
So the block DSS for p <= 3 (5.6x at p = 1, 1.35x at p = 3), the hand gather at p = 7.
The GB/s column is a convention: the minimum traffic (read and write every copy once).
Neither kernel moves only that: the gather also reads the other copies of shared nodes,
and the passes read and write the copies on element faces again after the z pass (all of
them at p = 1, 2 n^2 / n^3 = 1/4 at p = 7). Counting values accessed, that is about 3 N
for the passes against 2 N plus the shared copies for the gather; cache reuse decides
the actual memory traffic, which was not measured. So this is a hypothesis for why the
gather wins at p = 7, not a demonstrated cause. The DSS is about 20% of the p = 1 FCG
iteration (0.29 of 1.43 ms), so further DSS work is judged by the iteration time.

| | BP1 p = 1 | BP1 p = 3 | BP3 p = 3 | BP1 p = 7 (64^3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1/TEX throughput | 90% | 96% | 90% | 83% |
| DRAM throughput | 31% | 28% | 36% | 14% |
| registers, block size | 40, 256 | 64, 128 | 72, 64 | 212, 64 |
| achieved occupancy | 61% | 50% | 22% | 12% |

Every operator is bound by L1/shared memory, not DRAM: each sum-factorization pass goes through
shared memory, and that pipe is full at a third of the DRAM bandwidth. Also, none of their FP64
instructions is fused (BP1 p = 3: 428M FP64 instructions, 0 fma), so every contraction step is a
multiply and an add. The reference reaches DRAM speed with an even-odd factorization and FP64
tensor cores for p >= 3 (its Section 2), both of which cut the shared-memory traffic per DoF.

Step 1 (this commit): `--mir-cellwise-schedule` turns each contraction step out + a b into one
`math.fma` (BP1 p = 3: 120 fma and 1 multiply in the PTX, before 0 and 121). The BP gates pass
for bp1, bp3, bp1_tri and bp3_tri at p = 1, 3, 7 (the gate is a tolerance against direct sums,
so the new rounding is fine). The CVFEM operators of the same schedule are not regenerated yet.

GPU (df67598a, one GH200, 128^3 or 64^3 at p = 7, every run PASS except one, see below), operator ms
before -> after: BP1 p = 1 0.461-0.468 -> 0.466, p = 3 2.92-3.02 -> 2.920, p = 7 5.715 -> 5.569
(-2.6%); BP3 p = 3 8.435 -> 8.391, p = 7 16.760 -> 16.172 (-3.5%). Nsight Compute, BP1 p = 3: 3.79 ->
3.73 ms, L1/TEX 95.7% -> 96.3%. Half the arithmetic instructions buy almost nothing: the operators
are bound by L1/TEX requests, not by arithmetic. (BP3 p = 7 with DSS-Jacobi and no multigrid
stopped at the 2000-iteration limit, error 3.5e-10 against the 1e-10 test; that run had no
multigrid by mistake, not a wrong result.)

Where the L1/TEX requests come from (BP1 p = 3, the scheduled IR): the 1D matrices B and Bt are
kernel arguments in global memory, and every sweep re-reads them for every element and reduction
step. The x-sweep keeps a field value in a register for its 5 outputs but loads each B(i, j) once
per output: 5 matrix loads per field load. The y- and z-sweeps keep B(i, j) and read 5 field values
from shared memory. The reference's mass operator needs neither: its even-odd factorization
halves the matrices and its tensor-core tiles hold them in registers.

Step 2 (this commit): the matrices move to constant memory and every sweep becomes one thread
per 1D line. `--mir-cellwise-schedule` turns {mir.constant} arguments into globals
`<kernel>__m0 .. __m3` in the GPU's constant memory, which `GalerkinPtx` fills from the host's
matrices when their pointers change; with `max-block = q` a sweep keeps the field line in the
thread (constant inputs no longer count as reuse), and `unroll-reductions` unrolls the reduction
loops, so every matrix entry is read at a constant address (BP1 p = 3: 20 `ld.const` and no
`ld.global` of a matrix left in the PTX). All 12 BP gates pass, TSan clean at bp1 p = 3 and bp3
p = 7; filling the gate's B with Bt fails it. Shared memory per element +3-15%; launch shapes as
before except BP1 p = 7, one element per block instead of two (2 x 24976 B > 48 KB). The options
are opt-in: the CVFEM cell-wise operators keep their schedule (only the fma of step 1 changes
them; not regenerated yet).

GPU (bf087b34, one GH200, same runs as step 1), operator ms step 1 -> step 2: BP1 p = 1 0.466 ->
0.437 (-6%), p = 3 2.920 -> 2.373 (-19%), p = 7 5.569 -> 5.395 (-3%); BP3 p = 3 8.391 -> 6.859
(-18%), p = 7 16.172 -> 15.600 (-4%). BP1 operator + block DSS at p = 3: 15.95 GDoF/s (was 14.01;
the reference's A100 Table 7: 13.9). Nsight Compute, BP1 p = 3: L1/TEX 96% -> 67%, DRAM 28% -> 38%
(1789 GB/s on the timer, 45% of 4.0 TB/s); BP3 p = 3: DRAM 36% -> 47% (2148 GB/s). p = 3 is no longer
bound by L1. p = 7 is now bound by occupancy: one or two warps per block with 25-48 KB of shared
memory per element, achieved occupancy 12%, L1 23% and DRAM 15% (BP1).
The p-multigrid solve (128^3 p = 3, 64^3 p = 7, BoomerAMG, warm): 14 and 15 iterations as before
(true residuals 3.084e-11 and 2.628e-11, were 3.105e-11 and 2.793e-11: the rounding changed), solve

## BP3 p-multigrid (7cefa3e8, 643c9c68)

`--mg`: FCG preconditioned by one V-cycle p -> 3 -> 1 on the same mesh, the BP3 Galerkin form
rediscretized at each degree (MARSIR bp3_p{3,1}, q = p + 2, stored geometry), Chebyshev-Jacobi
(3,3) (the default is now (2,2), see the last subsection) through the DSS (equal pre and post:
a symmetric cycle), restriction of the raw residual, continuous corrections; on p = 1 BoomerAMG
(`--amg`, the assembled p = 1 matrix from the same operator) or Chebyshev steps (`--coarse`).
Reference `marsir-mlir/test/cellwise_bp_mg_ref.py`: transfers adjoint (2e-16), the fixed
V-cycle symmetric (3-5e-16) and positive definite on ne = 2; with an exact coarse solve, FCG to
a true residual of 1e-10 takes 8 / 8 / 8 iterations at p = 3, ne = 2 / 4 / 8 (Jacobi
20 / 45 / 93) and 8 / 9 at p = 7, ne = 2 / 4 (Jacobi 71 / 160).

GPU gate (ne = 4, Chebyshev-100 coarse, the reference's `--gpu` run): lmax, iterations,
the history at it 1 and 5, the true residual and the error equal the reference to every
printed digit at p = 3 (8 it) and p = 7 (9 it).

With BoomerAMG the iterations grow mildly with the mesh (one AMG cycle: p = 3, ne = 16 / 32
/ 64: 12 / 14 / 17; p = 7, ne = 8 / 16 / 32: 9 / 12 / 14); more AMG cycles bring them back
(p = 3, ne = 32: 14 / 10 / 8 iterations with 1 / 2 / 4 cycles), so the inexact coarse solve
is the cause.

Equal accuracy (16.8M cell-wise, GH200). At the same FCG tolerance the V-cycle runs stop 7-21x
less accurate (true residual 6.5e-10 to 1.9e-9 against Jacobi's 9e-11: FCG's sqrt(<r,z>/<r0,z0>)
depends on the preconditioner), so they ran at tol 3e-12; every one then reached a true

## Several ranks

`mars_marsir_bp` runs on any number of ranks (one GPU each). Each rank owns a sub-block of the
cube (`cellwise::Decomposition`); its tables hold its elements and the ghost elements around
them (`cellwise::block_elements`), and every DSS exchanges the shared copies with
`cellwise::UnstructuredHalo`, one per degree (p, and 3 and 1 for the multigrid). FCG sums its
dot products with `MPI_Allreduce`, the true residual and the checks are global, and BoomerAMG
gets the p = 1 halo and the world communicator. The power iteration of the multigrid starts
from a hash of each copy's global index (element id x n3 + node), which equals the local index
on one rank, so its start vector no longer depends on the partition. The block layout and the
generated DSS options stay on one rank.

Expected: the DSS gives the same bits on any rank count and only the global sums change, so
with DSS-Jacobi and with the V-cycle on Chebyshev-100 the history matches one rank to rounding.
BoomerAMG coarsens each partition differently, so its iterations may differ a little.

GPU gate (21dea96a). One rank repeats the (2,2) gate above to every printed digit (p = 3: 11 it,
p = 7: 12 it). On 1, 4 (2 x 2 x 1) and 8 (2 x 2 x 2) ranks, iterations and true residual:

### Smoothers for stretched elements: host study

`scripts/bp3_smoother_study.py`: the V-cycle of `marsir-mlir/test/cellwise_bp_mg_ref.py`
(p = 3 -> 1, Chebyshev (2,2), an exact p = 1 solve, so only the smoother varies) on the cube with
z divided by s. Chebyshev is applied to one of:

- point Jacobi (the GPU solver's smoother);
- element: additive Schwarz on element closures, the assembled operator on each element's free
  nodes, solved exactly;
- column along z: the same on columns of elements along the thin direction, exact line solves;
- column along x: lines across the thin direction, as a control;
- node lines: block Jacobi on lines of nodes. Elements are chained into lines along their thin
  axis (the local axis with the shortest mean edge, through faces that lie across the thin axis
  of both elements); every free node belongs to exactly one block, a run of nodes along its line,
  solved exactly with the assembled operator. This is the form that fits a GPU: banded blocks,
  about p + 1 numbers per node.

small eigenvalues of the preconditioned operator; with identical elements they cluster and FCG
removes them in a few iterations, with varying elements they spread. These host runs solve the
coarse level exactly; on the GPU, BoomerAMG on the p = 1 matrix may become the next limit once
the smoother is fixed (BoomerAMG alone needs 128 iterations on the wing's p = 1 matrix).

### Node-line smoother on the GPU (--smoother lines)

`solvers/mars_cellwise_lines.hpp` (`cellwise::NodeLines`; host part `mars_cellwise_lines_host.hpp`),
used by `mars_marsir_bp --mg --smoother lines` at p and, under p = 7, at 3. The host builds the
element lines of each rank's own elements (cut at rank boundaries) and, for every line element,
the lateral neighbours of its node lines (the element across each side face, the other elements
of each side edge) with their frames, matched by vertex ids. Each node goes to the rank that
counts it (one DSS of a code naming the counting copy); a chain proposes itself for its nodes and
the lowest chain wins (atomicMin), the reference's sequential claims. A block's matrix is the
assembled operator restricted to it: the assembled diagonal, and off it the line's own element and
its lateral neighbours on this rank, their line matrices sum-factorized from the stored geometry.
Neighbours on other ranks give the diagonal only, which keeps the block positive definite. One
thread per chain assembles and factors its blocks (banded Cholesky, bandwidth p) in a fixed order;
blocks are sorted by length and stored interleaved by warp. A smoothing step is three passes: DSS
of the residual onto the counting copies, one thread per block for the two banded triangular
solves (p values in registers each), a DSS of the solution with the Chebyshev update.

First version (83632260): the block from the line's own element matrices only, scaled
symmetrically to the assembled diagonal, so that no neighbour data was needed; on the host it
equalled the exact form on the four wing blocks. On the GPU it reproduced the reference to every
printed digit (ne 8, s = 100, `--mg --coarse 100`, tol 1e-10: 28 iterations, as
`bp3_smoother_study.py --gpu`; Jacobi 65, and 7 on boxes), but on the whole wing it was worse
than Jacobi: p = 3 1029 iterations (Jacobi 541), p = 7 2167 (904), with lmax 63 and 126 against
about 3: a few blocks with large eigenvalues of M^-1 A raise the Chebyshev interval [hi / 15, hi]
so far that the smoother does little anywhere else. The cause shows on the host with lines cut
every 4 elements, as rank boundaries cut them: lmax 74.6 and 131 iterations, against 2.69 and 31
for the exact form. Rows scaled by different factors (the rows at a cut lose a neighbour's share
of the diagonal) lose the near-zero row sums of a strongly coupled line, and the block gets nearly
singular modes the operator does not have. The four host blocks had no cuts. The own-line form
with the assembled diagonal and no scaling is safe (lmax 2.1-2.7) but weaker: wing blocks 35 / 27
/ 33 / 16 against exact 23 / 12 / 11 / 15.

Second version, exact: the neighbours' contributions as above. At an edge of valence 3 (an
irregular region of the wing), one lateral element borders two consecutive line elements and the
node line turns a corner in it; its dense element matrix couples the nodes before and after the
joint, which a sum over node-line segments misses (2% of a block's entries in the irregular wing
block, and positive definiteness is no longer certain). These couplings are added by quadrature
(element_entry). `scripts/test_node_lines.py` builds the tables with the C++ host code
(`tests/cellwise_lines_host_check.cpp`), emulates build_kernel step for step and compares every
band entry with the assembled operator: 1.1e-15 to 1.8e-15 relative on the cube (sheared, mixed
line directions, graded boxes) and on four wing blocks including the irregular one; a corrupted
face orientation bit fails (1.8e-2). The sum-factorized line matrix on all three axes and the
quadrature entry match probed element matrices to 2.2e-15 and 3.3e-16. Expected on the GPU, one
rank, ne 8, s = 100, `--mg --coarse 100`, tol 1e-10 (`bp3_smoother_study.py --gpu --smoothers
nodeline`): 12 iterations, lmax 2.9089, it 1 / 5 / 10 = 5.230568e-02 / 1.314450e-05 /
2.920982e-09 (deform 0.05); 7 iterations on boxes.

GPU, second version (373601b9): the one-rank reference runs print exactly these lines (12

### Node-line smoother generated by MARSIR (--lines-ptx)

`mlir_ir.emit_lines(p)`, through `marsir-mlir/test/dss_gpu_pipeline.py p --lines`, gives
`generated/lines_p{3,7}_sm90.ptx` with the three passes of a smoothing step: the residual as
`mir.dss` of b or b - y; the Chebyshev update (first0 / first / next) as structured loops, one
lane per copy, reading each copy's value at its counting copy by index as the hand update does;
the block solves as structured loops, one lane per block (`--mir-warp-per-element` with
`mir.lanes_per_element = 1`, 128 blocks per thread block), in the hand kernel's operation order.
`marsir::LinesPtx` (`marsir/mars_marsir_lines.hpp`) puts them behind `cellwise::LineKernels`;
`mars_marsir_bp --smoother lines --lines-ptx` runs them. Both forms are kept.

CPU gate `tools/emu_lines.cpp` (`--emulate`): every kernel bit for bit against the hand
formulas at p = 3 and 7, on the random tables with ghosts and Dirichlet entities and, for the
solve, a lane-interleaved layout over several warps; a wrong index in the reference fails (1167
of 3510 values). ThreadSanitizer (`--emu-tsan`) found a race in the first solve kernel: when the
block count is not a multiple of 128, the mapping clamps the surplus lanes onto the last block;
their stores are masked, but their reads of z race with that block's lane writing it. So the
layout is padded with zero-length blocks to a multiple of 128 (the kernel's contract; NodeLines
pads, the hand kernel ignores the padding); then TSan is clean.

GPU (GH200, f4a6d324): the generated passes give the hand runs' numbers in every printed digit.

p = 3: 262 iterations, the whole history, error 9.458e-10. Wing, p = 7, first 50 iterations:
lmax 3.2467, the history, the error after 50. Hand runs with the same settings of the cube at
p = 7 (40 iterations) and on 8 ranks (158) give the same counts and histories; at p = 7 the true
residual differs in the fourth digit (2.271e-12 generated, 2.275e-12 hand), at the rounding
