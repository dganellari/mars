# CEED BP1 / BP3 on cell-wise storage with flexible CG

The communication-free flexible CG of Wichrowski, "Coalesced Matrix-Free Finite Elements
in Cell-Wise Storage" (arXiv 2607.02335, Algorithm 1), with the CEED benchmark operators
generated by MARSIR from four-line specs.

- Operators: `marsir-compiler/specs/bp1.op` (mass, `v = g0*u`) and `bp3.op` (Laplacian,
  `dv = G du`, G the symmetric w det J J^-1 J^-T), `form = galerkin`, q = p + 2 Gauss
  points; PTX from `marsir-mlir/test/bp_gpu_pipeline.py` (cell-wise schedule), CPU gate
  `marsir-mlir/tools/emu_bp.cpp` against direct quadrature sums.
- Solver: `backend/distributed/unstructured/solvers/mars_cellwise_fcg.hpp`. x, p, z are
  continuous, r and q = A p unassembled; every scalar is a plain sum of a dual vector
  times a primal one (the paper's Lemma 1), so the DSS appears only in the
  preconditioner (DSS + Jacobi).
- Driver: `examples/distributed/unstructured/mars_marsir_bp.cu` (one GPU). Reference:
  `marsir-mlir/test/cellwise_bp_ref.py`.

## Correctness

numpy: cell-wise FCG and FCG on the assembled system give the same iterates (6e-16 over
25 iterations, BP1 and BP3); BP1 applied to 1 sums to the volume, BP3 applied to 1 is 0,
the BP3 element matrix is symmetric (1e-16), and the solve converges spectrally to the
manufactured solution (BP3 error 1e-4 at p = 3, 9e-6 at p = 5).

GPU (GH200, cstone 3a417b83): the FCG history equals the numpy one to every printed
digit (BP1 p = 3: 17 iterations; BP3 p = 3, 7, 1: 45, 71, 17), and every run passes
its checks (volume, null space, first elements against host quadrature, x = u).

## Throughput

GH200, one GPU, 16.8M element-local DoFs at every degree, deform 0.05, unique DoFs
(ne p + 1)^3. GB/s counts u, y and the stored geometry (1 value per Gauss point for BP1,
6 for BP3).

| GDoF/s per unique DoF | p = 1 | p = 3 | p = 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| BP1 operator | 4.62 (1552 GB/s) | 18.9 (1395) | 15.6 (627) |
| BP3 operator | 1.00 (1384 GB/s) | 6.55 (1678) | 5.37 (667) |
| DSS (unstructured gather) | 1.32 (165 GB/s) | 22.0 (821) | 91.0 (2145) |
| BP1 operator + DSS | 1.02 | 10.3 | 13.2 |
| BP3 operator + DSS | 0.56 | 5.12 | 5.04 |
| FCG + DSS-Jacobi iterations, BP1 / BP3 | 18 / 307 | 6 / 770 | 6 / 1225 |
| FCG iteration, BP1 / BP3 | 2.84 / 4.44 ms | 1.48 / 2.07 ms | 1.60 / 2.89 ms |

The paper (A100, 2.0 TB/s) reports for the whole mass operator on a general geometry
with its unstructured DSS path (Table 7): 2.7 / 12.3 GDoF/s at p = 1 / 3, and 16.0 at
p = 6. Our BP1 operator + DSS is below that on a GPU with about twice the bandwidth, so
there is headroom:

- DSS at p = 1: 165 GB/s. The hand unstructured gather runs one warp per element, and a
  p = 1 element has 8 values.
- The p = 7 operators: 627-667 GB/s. The generic cell-wise schedule keeps every sweep
  stage in shared memory (BP3: 42 KB per element), one warp per element.
- FCG: the iteration is 1.7-2x the operator + DSS. The vector updates and pairings are
  separate passes.
- BP3 iterations grow with the mesh and the degree under Jacobi, as expected; a
  multigrid preconditioner is the companion paper's step.

## DSS: MARSIR-generated against the hand gather

`--dss-ptx` (the DSS generated from `mir.dss`, bit-identical; every FCG history is the
same as with the hand gather). At p = 1 the generated kernel packs 16 elements into a
128-thread block, 8 lanes each; the hand gather uses a warp per element. At p = 3 both
use a warp per element.

| | p = 1 hand | p = 1 generated | p = 3 hand | p = 3 generated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DSS | 1.63 ms, 165 GB/s | 0.48 ms, 555 GB/s | 0.33 ms, 815 GB/s | 0.42 ms, 636 GB/s |
| BP1 operator + DSS (GDoF/s) | 1.03 | 2.28 | 10.2 | 8.98 |
| BP3 operator + DSS (GDoF/s) | 0.56 | 0.81 | 5.10 | 4.78 |
| FCG iteration, BP1 / BP3 | 2.85 / 4.45 ms | 1.64 / 3.26 ms | 1.49 / 2.05 ms | 1.60 / 2.14 ms |

So the generated DSS for p = 1 (3.4x) and the hand gather for p >= 3. The generated
kernel's 1.3x at p = 3 is the gap already measured at p = 7 (All-MARSIR form,
`cellwise_unstructured_dss.md`). BP1 p = 1 operator + DSS is now 2.28 GDoF/s, against
the paper's 2.7 (A100, Table 7, general geometry, unstructured DSS). In the BP1 p = 1
FCG iteration (1.64 ms), the operator takes 0.47 ms, the DSS 0.48 ms, and the vector
updates and pairings the remaining 0.69 ms (four passes, about 16 vector reads and
writes).

## Geometry: stored per Gauss point against trilinear from the corners

`--spec bp1_tri | bp3_tri` (9bbb398c): the kernel reads the element's 8 corners and
forms w det J (BP1) or w det J J^-1 J^-T (BP3) in registers at each Gauss point. Same
mesh and sizes as above (16.8M cell-wise, GH200). Every run PASS; the host oracle (from
the stored G) agrees to rounding, and iteration counts and histories match the stored
runs (BP3 final residuals differ in the 4th digit).

| operator, ms | p = 1 stored | p = 1 trilinear | p = 3 stored | p = 3 trilinear | p = 7 stored | p = 7 trilinear |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BP1 | 0.466 (1548 GB/s) | 1.005 | 0.383 (1384 GB/s) | 0.605 | 0.730 | 0.712 |
| BP3 | 2.153 (1387 GB/s) | 2.045 | 1.082 (1701 GB/s) | 1.316 | 2.140 | 2.012 |

Trilinear is faster only for BP3 at p = 1 and p = 7 (5-6%) and BP1 at p = 7 (2%), and
slower elsewhere (BP1 p = 1 2.2x). With the geometry gone the trilinear kernels move
137-668 GB/s, so they are no longer limited by bandwidth: the per-point geometry costs
more than reading it. The PTX shows the geometry adds about 170 (BP1) and 230 (BP3)
f64 instructions to the pointwise loop, all recomputed at every point, including the
corner differences that are constant for the element. What it saves is memory: G is
E 6 q^3 doubles for BP3 (2.7 GB at p = 1, 1.6 GB at p = 3) against 24 doubles per
element (0.4 GB, 50 MB).

### 21 per-element coefficients (02fb73e3)

The Jacobian of the trilinear map needs only 7 coefficients per component (the mixed
ones are shared by the three derivatives). A scalar flux computes these 21 from the
corners once per element, in every lane, and each J entry is then 3 FMAs at a point.
Phases, shared memory and warps per block are the same as stored at every p; no spills.
Same runs as above, one session for stored and the 21-coefficient form:

| operator, ms | stored | corners (9bbb398c) | 21 coefficients | registers stored / 21 coef. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BP1 p = 1 | 0.467 | 1.005 | 0.691 | 40 / 80 |
| BP1 p = 3 | 0.382 | 0.605 | 0.473 | 64 / 96 |
| BP1 p = 7 | 0.730 | 0.712 | 0.685 | 212 / 212 |
| BP3 p = 1 | 2.156 | 2.045 | 1.710 | 56 / 114 |
| BP3 p = 3 | 1.109 | 1.316 | 1.138 | 72 / 126 |
| BP3 p = 7 | 2.132 | 2.012 | 1.932 | 206 / 206 |

The coefficient form is faster than the corner form in every case. Against stored geometry,
it is faster for BP3 at p = 1 (21%) and p = 7 (9%) and BP1 at p = 7 (6%), equal within 3%
for BP3 at p = 3, and slower for BP1 at p = 1 (1.48x) and p = 3 (1.24x), where the stored
geometry is one value per point. FCG iteration, BP3: 4.45 -> 3.99 ms (p = 1), 2.07 -> 2.09
(p = 3), 2.89 -> 2.70 (p = 7). At p <= 3 the trilinear kernels still use about twice the
registers; whether that limits them is not measured.

## DSS: block-structured (passes inside blocks + block skeleton)

`--block B --rotate --block-dss` (227bf5a8, `solvers/mars_cellwise_block_dss.hpp`): the
elements stored in blocks of B^3, every block in a pseudo-random one of the 24 proper
rotations; a pass per axis inside the blocks (z out of place, x and y in place), then the
block skeleton from the unstructured tables built over the blocks. Same 16.8M cell-wise
vectors, BP1, GH200. Every run PASS; the block DSS equals the hand gather on a random
vector to 1.8e-15 relative, and iteration counts and histories match.

| DSS, ms | hand gather | block, B = 8 | block, B = 32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = 1 (ne 128) | 1.640 (164 GB/s) | 0.356 | 0.292 (64 blocks) |
| p = 3 (ne 64) | 0.320 | 0.259 | 0.237 (8 blocks) |
| p = 7 (ne 32) | 0.129 (2079 GB/s) | 0.177 | 0.157 (1 block, no skeleton) |

FCG iteration, BP1: p = 1 2.85 -> 1.43 ms (B = 32), p = 3 1.48 -> 1.38, p = 7 1.61 -> 1.63.
So the block DSS for p <= 3 (5.6x at p = 1, 1.35x at p = 3), the hand gather at p = 7.
The GB/s column is a convention: the minimum traffic (read and write every copy once).
Neither kernel moves only that: the gather also reads the other copies of shared nodes,
and the passes read and write the copies on element faces again after the z pass (all of
them at p = 1, 2 n^2 / n^3 = 1/4 at p = 7). Counting values accessed, that is about 3 N
for the passes against 2 N plus the shared copies for the gather; cache reuse decides
the actual memory traffic, which was not measured. So this is a hypothesis for why the
gather wins at p = 7, not a demonstrated cause. The DSS is about 20% of the p = 1 FCG
iteration (0.29 of 1.43 ms), so further DSS work is judged by the iteration time.

### One thread per block node (920754ee)

Instead of the three passes, one thread per block node sums the node's copies in the
block and writes the sum to each (every copy read once, written once), then the same
skeleton. B = 32, rotated, BP1, one session (the B = 8 runs of this session were lost
to a file-name error in the run command):

| B = 32 | DSS passes | DSS block nodes | FCG iteration passes | FCG iteration block nodes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p = 1 | 0.292 ms | 0.221 ms | 1.432 ms | 1.363 ms |
| p = 3 | 0.239 ms | 0.133 ms | 1.379 ms | 1.258 ms |
| p = 7 | 0.159 ms | 0.145 ms | 1.642 ms | 1.621 ms |

Block nodes beat the passes at every degree; all PASS, equal to the hand gather to
2.7e-15. Against the hand gather (earlier session, B = 8 layout): FCG iteration p = 1
2.85 -> 1.36 ms, p = 3 1.48 -> 1.26 ms; at p = 7 the gather's 0.129 ms DSS and 1.61 ms
iteration are within 2% of block nodes, not measured in the same session.

B = 8, rotated, BP1, hand gather and both block stages in one session:

| B = 8 | DSS hand / passes / nodes (ms) | FCG iteration hand / passes / nodes (ms) |
|---|---|---|
| p = 1 | 1.648 / 0.355 / 0.269 | 2.852 / 1.500 / 1.410 |
| p = 3 | 0.321 / 0.260 / 0.168 | 1.480 / 1.413 / 1.300 |
| p = 7 | 0.128 / 0.176 / 0.173 | 1.605 / 1.663 / 1.656 |

So block nodes for p <= 3 (larger blocks better: B = 32 gives 0.221 and 0.133 ms), and
the hand gather at p = 7, where it is 3% faster per FCG iteration than either block stage.

### Block nodes generated by MARSIR (c588deba)

`--block-dss generated`: mir.block_sum + mir.block_skeleton through
`marsir-mlir/test/block_dss_gpu_pipeline.py`. On the GPU the result equals the hand
block nodes bit for bit (0 copies differ) in every run, and every FCG solve matches the
hand history. Runs on clean nodes (bad nodes nid005617/5669/5742 excluded; nid005669
gave illegal-address crashes and one wrong solve in the first round, for hand and
generated kernels alike):

| DSS, ms (FCG iteration, ms) | hand block nodes | generated |
|---|---|---|
| p = 1, B = 8 | 0.271 (1.423) | 0.267 (1.410) |
| p = 1, B = 32 | 0.220 (1.364) | 0.205 (1.348) |
| p = 3, B = 8 | 0.169 (1.300) | 0.212 (1.354) |

The generated form equals or beats the hand kernels at p = 1 and is 1.2-1.4x slower at
p >= 3 (2-4% of the FCG iteration at p = 3; at p = 7 the hand gather is used anyway).
The cause of the gap is not measured.

ncu at p = 3, B = 32 put the gap in block_sum (generated 104.4M instructions, 48
registers, 200 us against the hand kernel's 68.9M, 37, 143 us; the skeleton 13.5 vs
12.2 us). The lowering did the per-axis copy arithmetic in 64-bit integers; in 32 bits
(840d1c28, one 64-bit division per item) the generated DSS at p = 3 is 0.162 -> 0.149 ms
(B = 32) and 0.212 -> 0.189 ms (B = 8), against 0.134 / 0.169 ms by hand: 1.11-1.12x,
FCG iteration 1.282 / 1.323 ms against 1.264 / 1.300 (1.4-1.8%). Bits still identical.
ncu after the change (p = 3, B = 32): block_sum 85.1M instructions (was 104.4M; hand
68.9M), 42 registers (hand 37), 167 us (hand 143 us); the skeleton 12.26 us (hand 12.22).
The remaining 1.24x instructions in block_sum are not attributed (the copy loops are
dynamic scf.for nests; the hand kernel's SASS was not compared). Left there: about 1.4%
of the FCG iteration at p = 3, where the hand block nodes are used anyway.

## BP3 p-multigrid (7cefa3e8, 643c9c68)

`--mg`: FCG preconditioned by one V-cycle p -> 3 -> 1 on the same mesh, the BP3 Galerkin form
rediscretized at each degree (MARSIR bp3_p{3,1}, q = p + 2, stored geometry), Chebyshev-Jacobi
(3,3) (the default is now (2,2), see the last subsection) through the DSS (equal pre and post:
a symmetric cycle), restriction of the raw residual, continuous corrections; on p = 1 BoomerAMG
(`--amg`, the assembled p = 1 matrix from the same operator) or Chebyshev steps (`--coarse`).
Reference `marsir-mlir/test/cellwise_bp_mg_ref.py`: transfers adjoint (2e-16), the fixed
V-cycle symmetric (3-5e-16) and positive definite on ne = 2; with an exact coarse solve, FCG to
a true residual of 1e-10 takes 8 / 8 / 8 iterations at p = 3, ne = 2 / 4 / 8 (Jacobi
20 / 45 / 93) and 8 / 9 at p = 7, ne = 2 / 4 (Jacobi 71 / 160).

GPU gate (ne = 4, Chebyshev-100 coarse, the reference's `--gpu` run): lmax, iterations,
the history at it 1 and 5, the true residual and the error equal the reference to every
printed digit at p = 3 (8 it) and p = 7 (9 it).

With BoomerAMG the iterations grow mildly with the mesh (one AMG cycle: p = 3, ne = 16 / 32
/ 64: 12 / 14 / 17; p = 7, ne = 8 / 16 / 32: 9 / 12 / 14); more AMG cycles bring them back
(p = 3, ne = 32: 14 / 10 / 8 iterations with 1 / 2 / 4 cycles), so the inexact coarse solve
is the cause.

Equal accuracy (16.8M cell-wise, GH200). At the same FCG tolerance the V-cycle runs stop 7-21x
less accurate (true residual 6.5e-10 to 1.9e-9 against Jacobi's 9e-11: FCG's sqrt(<r,z>/<r0,z0>)
depends on the preconditioner), so they ran at tol 3e-12; every one then reached a true
residual at or below Jacobi's and a smaller error:

| BP3 | Jacobi: it, true residual, error, solve | V-cycle + AMG x1 | V-cycle + AMG x2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = 1 (ne 128) | 307, 9.2e-11, 5.2e-12, 1363 ms | 23, 3.3e-11, 6.1e-13, 202 + 398 ms setup | 16, 2.3e-11, 5.2e-13, 175 + 406 ms |
| p = 3 (ne 64) | 770, 8.5e-11, 3.2e-12, 1586 ms | 19, 3.2e-11, 6.0e-13, 272 + 407 ms | 13, 7.7e-11, 9.3e-13, 197 + 416 ms |
| p = 7 (ne 32) | 1225, 9.2e-11, 2.3e-12, 3531 ms | 16, 7.0e-11, 1.5e-12, 363 + 477 ms | 12, 1.6e-11, 2.4e-13, 282 + 461 ms |

With two AMG cycles the solve is 7.8x (p = 1), 8.0x (p = 3) and 12.5x (p = 7) faster than
Jacobi, 2.3x / 2.6x / 4.8x with the setup (power iterations and AMG setup, once per matrix).
Two cycles beat one at every degree at (3,3); the defaults are in the last subsection. An FCG iteration now
costs 10.9 / 15.2 / 23.5 ms (Jacobi 4.4 / 2.1 / 2.9 ms): the V-cycle, mostly its operator
applies on the fine level.

### Smoothing steps and AMG cycles

Correction to 98d8c275: its (1,1) and (2,2) runs had one AMG cycle, not two. That binary was
older than 2a00e840 and still had the default of one cycle; an explicit `--amg-cycles 1` rerun of
(1,1) gives the same result to every digit. The driver now prints the cycles (`coarse BoomerAMG
x3`).

3531 ms and 9.2e-11 at p = 7):

|---|---|---|---|
| AMG x1 | 161 ms, 23 it, 6.2e-11 | 202 ms, 19 it, 6.8e-11 | 272 ms, 19 it, 3.2e-11 |
| AMG x2 | | 163 ms, 14 it, 3.2e-11 | 197 ms, 13 it, 7.7e-11 |
| AMG x3 | | **159 ms**, 13 it, 8.0e-11 | 177 ms, 11 it, 2.0e-11 |
| AMG x4 | | 166 ms, 13 it, 3.9e-11 | 170 ms, 10 it, 9.0e-12 |

| p = 7, ne 32 | (1,1) | (2,2) | (3,3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AMG x1 | 269 ms, 26 it, 8.5e-11 | 284 ms, 17 it, 4.5e-11 | 363 ms, 16 it, 7.0e-11 |
| AMG x2 | | 241 ms, 14 it, 1.0e-10 (above Jacobi's) | 282 ms, 12 it, 1.6e-11 |
| AMG x3 | | **245 ms**, 14 it, 4.4e-11 | 267 ms, 11 it, 1.5e-11 |
| AMG x4 | | 233 ms, 13 it, 1.5e-10 (above Jacobi's) | 248 ms, 10 it, 8.3e-11 |

The two (1,1) x1 runs differ by 1% (161 / 162 ms, 266 / 269 ms). A V-cycle makes 2k operator
applies on the fine level (k - 1 before, one for the residual, k after) and FCG one more; one AMG
cycle adds 0.5-0.9 ms to an iteration.

With an exact coarse solve (reference, to a true residual of 1e-10):

| exact p = 1 solve | (1,1) | (2,2) | (3,3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = 3, ne 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 | 12 / 16 / 19 / 20 | 11 / 12 / 12 / 12 | 8 / 8 / 8 / 8 |
| p = 7, ne 2 / 4 | 20 / 23 | 12 / 13 | 8 / 9 |

That is about 60 fine-level applies for every k at p = 3 (20 x 3, 12 x 5, 8 x 7). With one AMG
cycle the stronger smoothers lose more to the inexact coarse solve (19 iterations against 8 at
(3,3), 19 against 12 at (2,2), 23 against 20 at (1,1)). With more cycles (2,2) x3 and (3,3) x4
come within 1-2 iterations of their exact-coarse counts and are the fastest. The optimum is flat:
per decade of true residual, (2,2) x3 / x4 and (3,3) x3 / x4 take 15.4-16.5 ms at p = 3 and
23.6-24.6 ms at p = 7, against 19.5 / 26.1 ms for (3,3) x2 and 15.8 / 26.7 ms for (1,1) x1.

(2,2) x2 takes about the same time per decade as x3 (15.5 / 24.1 ms against 15.7 / 23.6 ms), but
at p = 7 it stopped above Jacobi's residual. AMG alone at p = 1 (ne 128; Jacobi 1363 ms, 9.2e-11):
x1 202 ms / 23 it / 3.3e-11, x2 175 / 16 / 2.3e-11, x3 173 / 13 / 1.1e-11, x4 171 / 11 / 9.3e-12.

Defaults now: (2,2) and 3 AMG cycles at every degree. That is the fastest solve at equal accuracy
at p = 3 and 7 (159 ms and 245 ms: 1.24x / 1.15x faster than (3,3) x2, 10.0x / 14.4x faster than
Jacobi, 2.9x / 4.9x with the setup) and 1% from the best at p = 1 (173 ms, 7.9x faster than
Jacobi). GPU gate of this default (ne = 4, Chebyshev-100 coarse): lmax, iterations, the history
at it 1 and 5, the true residual and the error equal the reference to every printed digit at
p = 3 (11 it) and p = 7 (12 it). The default run at p = 3, ne 64 prints `Chebyshev (2,2), coarse
BoomerAMG x3` and repeats the explicit run to every digit.
